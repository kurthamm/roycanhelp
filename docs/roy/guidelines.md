# Roy's vision and guidelines (baseline)

Built only from what Roy has said and done: the site text he approved, his About page, 68 messages to his editor (Aug 3 to Sep 7, 2026), and his commits. Anything not on this page is an open question for Roy, not a decision. Update this file whenever Roy states a new preference.

## The vision, in his words
- "A field guide written by someone who's been in the waiting room and lived to tell the tale."
- "The bureaucracy is a system, not a mystery. It has inputs, outputs, failure modes, and undocumented behavior." "Specs beat opinions."
- "I'm not selling anything. There are no affiliate links." Links go to official government websites, not to providers he recommends.
- "Satire punches up at bureaucracies and broken systems, never at children, families, grief, or disability. Grief and love are played straight."
- "I don't think having a child with a disability is a blessing. I think having a child is a blessing."
- "Information is accurate or linked to the source." "This site needs to be accurate."
- Honest about his limits: "All of my experience is in South Carolina. The federal stuff translates to anyone." He apologized that his main body of knowledge is South Carolina and autism.
- Not medical or legal advice; not grief counseling; not comprehensive.

## Rules he has set (each came from a request)
1. People-first language, everywhere: "a child with autism," never "autistic child" (Aug 10).
2. Never the word "kid" for a child (Aug 12).
3. Not the phrase "inspiration porn", nothing with the word "porn" (Aug 4).
4. Clean language for a family audience (no profanity, no crude jokes).
5. No em dashes in site text (Kurt's rule; Roy's voice avoids them).
6. His own words and his own stories. When the editor invented a fun fact instead of using his, he objected (Aug 4). When an AI-written paragraph was wrong, he called it "total bullocks" and had it removed (Sep 7).
7. Removed on purpose, never to return: the "I spent money on things that didn't exist or didn't work" section (Aug 10); the first paragraph of "How Grandma's Generosity Can Cost Your Child Everything" (Sep 7); "inspiration porn" (Aug 4).
8. Page names must match what the page covers (he renamed a page "Ages 14 to 26" on Aug 12 and the old title is not coming back).
9. Never mention Kurt on the site; credit and responsibility wording is settled in the legal pages.

## What he values (patterns in his edits)
- Concrete, usable tips from real life: recording every IEP meeting, the SC Autism Society's Parent School Partnership Program, aging off insurance at 26, a medical-history document kept current.
- Accuracy and the right program names; plain explanations of how to push back with evidence.
- Seeing where a change landed (he asked "where did you put that?" and "I refreshed and don't see it").
- Fixing what is wrong rather than leaving it, and removing what is unreliable.

## What additions must be
- Additive only. Never rewrite text Roy added; never restore text he removed (enforced by `tools/roy-guard.mjs`).
- Built from his own pages and verified official sources, never invented. Facts checked at the source and dated.
- In his spirit: plain, practical, first-person where it is his, no marketing language, no selling.
- Visible to him: the editor tells him what others changed so he can correct it, and his decision stands.

## Open questions only Roy can answer (do not guess)
Look and feel of the site (he has not seen the new design), photos or a voice clip, a newsletter, a podcast, other states, how he wants to be credited off-site.
