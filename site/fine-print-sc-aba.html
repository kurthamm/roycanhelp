South Carolina Fine Print: ABA Therapy and Medicaid

In short: South Carolina Medicaid covers autism treatment, including ABA, for members 0 to 21 with an ASD diagnosis who meet medical necessity. The diagnosis has to come from a comprehensive testing report by a psychologist, licensed psychoeducational specialist or developmental pediatrician, the provider requests prior authorization, and SCDHHS says providers often have waiting lists.

ABA is one of the first things parents ask about after a diagnosis, and the answer in South Carolina has a few steps that are easy to trip on. This page is about South Carolina only. Here is what SCDHHS says on its own pages, word for word.

Medicaid Covers ABA for Children, up to Age 21

If your child has an autism diagnosis and Medicaid, South Carolina covers therapy, including ABA. The state spells out who qualifies.

The state says: “Any member 0-21, diagnosed with ASD and who meets medical necessity, is eligible for ASD treatment services.” (SCDHHS, Autism Spectrum Disorder Services, FAQs for Members)

The state says: “Medicaid covers applied behavior analysis (ABA) as well as other evidence-based treatments.” (SCDHHS, Autism Spectrum Disorder Services, FAQs for Members)

That is for children on Healthy Connections Medicaid. Private insurance is a different law, covered on the South Carolina law page.

The Diagnosis Paper Has to Come From the Right Person

This is a step that is easy to miss. SCDHHS wants the diagnosis documented in a particular way.

The state says: “In order to meet medical necessity for ASD services, members age 0-21 need to have a diagnosis of ASD based on a comprehensive testing report from a psychologist, licensed psychoeducational specialist (LPES) or developmental pediatrician that indicates that ASD treatment services are necessary for their behavioral health care.” (SCDHHS, Autism Spectrum Disorder Services, FAQs for Members)

So if the diagnosis came from someone else, or without a full testing report, ask the provider what Medicaid needs before you wait. The state page lists where to find these professionals.

Say: “What diagnostic report does Medicaid require for ABA services for my child, and does the one we have meet it? If not, who can do the comprehensive testing?”

The Provider Asks for the Approval, Not You

You do not file a prior authorization yourself. The provider does.

The state says: “ASD treatment providers are responsible for obtaining prior authorization for any assessment or ASD treatment services.” (SCDHHS, Autism Spectrum Disorder Services, FAQs for Members)

Which means the first call is to a provider that takes Medicaid, and the provider handles the paperwork. If your child is in a managed care plan, the same services apply.

The state says: “Yes. Contact your MCO about receiving services for your member ages 0-21.” (SCDHHS, Autism Spectrum Disorder Services, FAQs for Members)

The Waitlist Is Real, and the State Says So

Parents tell each other that providers are full. The state agrees.

The state says: “Nationwide, and in South Carolina, there is a need for increased capacity of ASD providers. It is not uncommon for providers to have a waiting list for services.” (SCDHHS, Autism Spectrum Disorder Services, FAQs for Members)

So call several, ask to be added to every list, and ask each one how long the wait is and how they will contact you. A therapy waitlist is not a no. Ask Medicaid for the provider search tool and keep going down it.

A Way to Ask That Works

Short and kind wins. Say who your child is, that Medicaid is the payer, and what you need, and then send it in an email so there is a date on it. Family Connection of South Carolina keeps a therapy directory and can help you find providers. Roy's Therapies page covers the waitlist problem from the family side.
