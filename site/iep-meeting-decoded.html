The IEP Meeting, Decoded: Who Gets a Chair at the Table

An IEP meeting can feel like walking into a room where everyone else got the memo. They did not get a better memo. They got the same federal rule you can read, and it says more about your seat at the table than you might expect.

The Required Chairs

Federal law says the IEP team has to include these people (34 CFR 300.321):

  • You. The parents of the child are members of the team, not guests of it.
  • A regular education teacher, if your child is or may be in the regular classroom.
  • A special education teacher or provider who works with your child.
  • A school district representative who is qualified to provide or supervise specially designed instruction, knows the general curriculum, and knows what resources the district has.
  • Someone who can interpret the evaluation results, in plain terms for the team. This can be one of the people above.
  • Your child, whenever appropriate.

If a chair is empty, it is fair to ask why, and to ask for the meeting to be rescheduled when the right people can be there.

The Chair You Get to Add

The rule also lets you or the school invite other people "who have knowledge or special expertise regarding the child" (34 CFR 300.321). That can be a therapist, a relative, an advocate, or anyone who knows your child well. The school can add people too, so the meeting notice has to say who will be there (34 CFR 300.322). Ask for that list ahead of time, and tell them who you are bringing.

Scheduling: A Mutually Agreed Time and Place

The school has to tell you about the meeting early enough that you can attend, and it has to schedule it at a mutually agreed on time and place (34 CFR 300.322). "Mutually agreed" means it is not enough to announce a date and call it done. If you cannot be there in person, the rule allows you to take part by phone or video. A meeting can go ahead without a parent only when the school is unable to convince you to attend.

How Often: At Least Once a Year

The team has to review your child's IEP periodically, but not less than annually, to see whether the goals are being met, and revise it when they are not (34 CFR 300.324). The School Years page covers asking for a meeting sooner when something is not working.

Reevaluation: Every Three Years, Unless You Both Say Otherwise

A reevaluation can happen no more than once a year unless you and the school agree otherwise, and it has to happen at least once every three years unless you and the school agree it is unnecessary (34 CFR 300.303). Notice the pattern: in both directions, the exception needs your agreement.

Three Things Worth Saying

  • "Please send me the names and roles of everyone who will attend."
  • "I would like to bring someone who knows my child. Who else will be there?"
  • "Please put what the team decided in writing." If the answer is no to something you asked for, the disagree-in-writing guide shows what happens next.
