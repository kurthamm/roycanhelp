South Carolina Fine Print: BabyNet (Early Intervention)

In short: In South Carolina, a parent can refer their own child to BabyNet without a doctor's referral, and SCDHHS says the program provides services for free for any eligible child. Eligibility rests on an assessment of the child's skills, BabyNet is part of the state Medicaid agency's Healthy Connections program, and a formal complaint must be filed within one year of the alleged violation.

This page is about South Carolina only. Roy lived this here, and the state describes BabyNet on its own pages. Here is what SCDHHS says, word for word, so you can point to it.

You Can Refer Your Child Yourself, and It Is Free

South Carolina's Early Intervention program is called BabyNet. Many parents wait for a doctor to bring it up. They do not have to.

SCDHHS says: “If a provider does not refer you, you can reach out yourself. A doctor’s referral is not needed.” (scdhhs.gov/babynet)

The BabyNet page has an online referral form and a phone number for the Central Referral team. And on cost, South Carolina says it plainly.

SCDHHS says: “This publicly funded program provides services for free for any child who is eligible.” (scdhhs.gov/babynet)

That matches what Roy found. The federal rules let some other states charge families for certain services, so the Early Intervention fine print explains the difference. In South Carolina, the state's own page says free.

Say: “I would like to refer my child to BabyNet. I do not have a doctor's referral. What do you need from me to start?”

Who Qualifies

BabyNet decides eligibility by assessing the child, not by waiting for a diagnosis.

SCDHHS says: “Eligibility is based on an assessment of a child’s skills.” (scdhhs.gov/babynet)

SCDHHS says: “If your child has a significant developmental delay or mild/moderate delays in two areas of development, they may qualify for BabyNet. If a child has a physical or mental condition that is likely to result in a developmental delay, they may be eligible too.” (scdhhs.gov/babynet)

If you are not sure whether your child's delay is big enough, make the referral anyway and let the assessment answer it. The federal evaluation fine print covers what the law says about suspecting a disability.

What Happens After You Call

The first step is an intake appointment, and it is longer than people expect.

SCDHHS says: “An intake appointment may take up to 1.5 hours.” (scdhhs.gov/babynet)

Bring what you have. The referral asks for documents that are easy to gather in advance.

SCDHHS says: “Reason for referral and any known concerns All available related documents Evaluations Medical records Developmental tracking Screenings” (scdhhs.gov/babynet)

Roy's Birth to Three page covers the plan that comes next.

It Is Run by SCDHHS, Not Who You Might Expect

Parents often look for BabyNet in the wrong place. It is part of the state Medicaid agency's Healthy Connections program.

SCDHHS says: “Effective July 1, 2017, BabyNet is part of the South Carolina Department of Health and Human Services' Healthy Connections program.” (scdhhs.gov, BabyNet for families)

The page also notes that BabyNet is South Carolina's Part C program, which is the federal law the Early Intervention fine print explains.

When Something Goes Wrong: Three Steps, and a One Year Deadline

South Carolina lays out the steps for a concern. The first two are conversations: talk to the service coordinator or their supervisor, then ask for a meeting of the IFSP team. If that does not work, there are formal options.

SCDHHS says: “The complaint must be filed within one year of the alleged violation of rights or regulations.” (scdhhs.gov, disputes and concerns)

There are three formal routes. A written complaint asks the state to investigate.

SCDHHS says: “Written formal state complaints are filed when an investigation is requested to determine if IDEA has been followed.” (scdhhs.gov, disputes and concerns)

Mediation is voluntary.

SCDHHS says: “Mediation is a voluntary process under Part C of IDEA that brings the parent and others together to resolve disagreements.” (scdhhs.gov, disputes and concerns)

And a due process hearing is available to parents.

SCDHHS says: “A due process hearing can be requested when the parent wants an officer of the court to make a legally binding decision about whether IDEA has been followed.” (scdhhs.gov, disputes and concerns)

The state asks people to use its Request for Dispute Resolution form, which is linked from that page. The disagree-in-writing guide has the general approach, and the letters page has one for the 45 day timeline.

A Way to Ask That Works

Start with the friendly step: call the service coordinator, say what you need, and follow up by email so there is a record. Most coordinators carry a heavy caseload, and a clear request is easy to act on. Family Connection of South Carolina offers free help if you want someone to look over what you are planning. Check the SCDHHS pages linked below, since phone numbers and forms change.
