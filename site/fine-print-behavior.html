The Fine Print on Behavior and Discipline at School

A call from school about behavior can send any parent into a spin. Here is what the federal rules actually say for a child with an IEP, with the exact sentences so you can point to them, and calm things to say.

Ten School Days, and Then It Counts

Being sent home is not the same as being told what the rules allow. For a child with an IEP, the number of days matters, and the school is supposed to keep track.

The rule says: “The removal is for more than 10 consecutive school days” (34 CFR 300.536)

It is not only one long stretch. A series of shorter removals can add up, if they form a pattern.

The rule says: “The child has been subjected to a series of removals that constitute a pattern” (34 CFR 300.536)

Once removals pass that point, the school has to follow a different set of steps. Keep your own list of dates, because the count is easy to lose.

Say: “Could you tell me how many days my child has been out of class this school year, including any sent home early or picked up?”

Learning Does Not Stop When the Child Is Out

After the tenth day in a school year, the school has a duty to keep teaching.

The rule says: “Continue to receive educational services” (34 CFR 300.530)

The rule goes on to say what that means: enough to keep taking part in the general curriculum, even in another setting, and to keep working toward the goals in the IEP. Ask what the plan is, who is teaching, and how many hours.

Say: “My child has now been out more than ten days. What services will he or she receive, who will provide them, and how many hours a week?”

A Meeting Within Ten School Days

When the school decides to change a child's placement because of a conduct violation, there is a meeting about one question: was the behavior connected to the disability? You are part of that meeting.

The rule says: “Within 10 school days of any decision to change the placement of a child with a disability because of a violation of a code of student conduct, the LEA, the parent, and relevant members of the child's IEP Team (as determined by the parent and the LEA) must review all relevant information in the student's file” (34 CFR 300.530)

The team looks at two questions. Was the behavior caused by, or closely related to, the disability? Or was it the direct result of the school not following the IEP?

The rule says: “If the conduct in question was caused by, or had a direct and substantial relationship to, the child's disability” (34 CFR 300.530)

The rule says: “If the conduct in question was the direct result of the LEA's failure to implement the IEP” (34 CFR 300.530)

You and the team choose which members attend, so bring what you know. Roy's School Years page and the IEP Meeting, Decoded page help with the room itself.

If the Answer Is Yes

If the team finds the behavior was a manifestation of the disability, the response is supposed to be support, not punishment. The team looks at the behavior plan or creates one.

The rule says: “Conduct a functional behavioral assessment, unless the LEA had conducted a functional behavioral assessment before the behavior that resulted in the change of placement occurred, and implement a behavioral intervention plan for the child” (34 CFR 300.530)

And the child generally goes back to where they were.

The rule says: “return the child to the placement from which the child was removed, unless the parent and the LEA agree to a change of placement as part of the modification of the behavioral intervention plan” (34 CFR 300.530)

There are special circumstances (weapons, illegal drugs, or serious bodily injury) where a school may remove a child to an interim alternative educational setting for up to 45 school days without regard to whether the behavior was a manifestation of the disability. If your situation involves one of those, call your state parent center right away.

Support Comes First

The best answer to behavior is not a rule about suspension. It is a plan that helps before things go wrong. The IEP team has a specific duty here.

The rule says: “In the case of a child whose behavior impedes the child's learning or that of others, consider the use of positive behavioral interventions and supports, and other strategies, to address that behavior” (34 CFR 300.324)

Say: “My child's behavior is getting in the way of learning. Could the team consider positive behavioral supports and a functional behavioral assessment at the next meeting?”

They Have to Tell You the Same Day

When the school decides on a removal that counts as a change of placement, it owes you notice and your rights, not a surprise.

The rule says: “the LEA must notify the parents of that decision, and provide the parents the procedural safeguards notice described in § 300.504” (34 CFR 300.530)

If you have not gotten that notice, ask for it in writing. The school fine print page has more on the notice and your records.

A Way to Ask That Works

Hard days at school happen, and the staff are often as worn out as the family. Keep it calm and specific: what happened, what you are asking for, and a thank you. Ask for answers in writing. If you need to disagree, the disagree-in-writing guide shows the next steps.
