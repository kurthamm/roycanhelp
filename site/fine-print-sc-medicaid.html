South Carolina Fine Print: Medicaid, Waivers and Katie Beckett

In short: South Carolina Medicaid has a Katie Beckett (TEFRA) option that counts only the child's income and resources, for a child 18 or under who meets an institutional level of care. SCDHHS lists seven active waivers by name, shows the Pervasive Developmental Disorder waiver as inactive, and sends appeals to its Office of Appeals and Hearings.

This page is about South Carolina only. The federal rules apply everywhere, but the programs, names and limits are each state's own. Here is what the South Carolina Department of Health and Human Services (SCDHHS) says on its own pages, word for word.

South Carolina Has a Katie Beckett (TEFRA) Option

Families often hear “you make too much” and stop there. South Carolina Medicaid has a program for children with disabilities that looks at the child's income and not the family's. The TEFRA / Katie Beckett page explains the federal rule behind it. This is how SCDHHS describes it.

SCDHHS says: “Healthy Connections provides benefits to certain children with disabilities who would not ordinarily be eligible because their parents' income and/or resources exceed the limit.” (scdhhs.gov, program eligibility)

SCDHHS lists the conditions. A child must be 18 or under, live at home, live in South Carolina, meet the SSI definition of disability, and meet an institutional level of care. As of 2026 the page gives these limits, which change every year:

SCDHHS says: “Have gross monthly income below $2,982 and countable resources at or below $2,000 (only the child's income and resources are counted)” (scdhhs.gov, program eligibility)

A level of care sounds like the child has to be in an institution. SCDHHS says it does not.

SCDHHS says: “Meeting a level of care does not mean a child must be institutionalized.” (scdhhs.gov, program eligibility)

What you get, and what you do not, is spelled out too.

SCDHHS says: “A child who meets these requirements is eligible for the full range of Medicaid covered services. If the child needs special services, which are available only under the home and community based waiver, they must apply for and be accepted into one of the waiver programs to receive these special services.” (scdhhs.gov, program eligibility)

Say: “My child has a disability and our income is over the Medicaid limit. Is my child eligible for the TEFRA (Katie Beckett) program, and what is the application and level-of-care process?”

The page lists a TEFRA application form and fact sheet, and it points families to Family Connection of South Carolina for support.

The Waivers by Name, and the Ones That Are Closed

The waivers are where the home and community services live, and a family usually needs the program's name before anyone will talk to them. SCDHHS lists the active ones and says where to read who is eligible and how to apply.

SCDHHS says: “Currently Approved Waivers Community Choices (CC) Waiver (SC.0405) Community Supports (CS) Waiver (SC.0676) Head and Spinal Cord Injury (HASCI) Waiver (SC.0284) HIV/AIDS Waiver (SC.0186) Mechanical Ventilator Dependent (Vent) Waiver (SC.40181) Medically Complex Children (MCC) Waiver (SC.0675) Intellectual Disability and Related Disabilities (ID/RD) Waiver (SC.0237)” (scdhhs.gov, waivers)

SCDHHS says: “Descriptions of these waiver programs, who is eligible and how you can apply are available on the waiver overview chart” (scdhhs.gov, waivers)

For families of a child with autism, one line on that page matters. A waiver many families have heard of is no longer open.

SCDHHS says: “The following waivers are currently inactive: Pervasive Development Disorder (PDD), Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility (PRTF), Community Engagement and Children's Health Access In Community Environments (CHANCE).” (scdhhs.gov, waivers)

So when someone says to look into the autism waiver, ask which one they mean. Two of the active waivers are proposed for renewal, which can change who is eligible.

SCDHHS says: “The proposed effective date for these waivers is Jan. 1, 2027: Medically Complex Children (MCC) Waiver (SC.0675) Intellectual Disability and Related Disabilities (ID/RD) Waiver (SC.0237)” (scdhhs.gov, waivers)

Say: “Which South Carolina waivers could my child qualify for, which are open, and how do I get on the waiting list today even if we do not need services yet?”

Roy's Paying for It page explains why getting on a list early matters.

If Medicaid Says No: Where the Hearing Goes

A denial is not the last word. SCDHHS has an office for appeals, and an appeal means an in-person hearing.

SCDHHS says: “The SCDHHS Office of Appeals and Hearings oversees appeals filed by either an applicant, member, or provider from an adverse agency decision.” (scdhhs.gov, appeals)

SCDHHS says: “When you file an appeal, you are requesting an in-person hearing before a hearing officer.” (scdhhs.gov, appeals)

Your deadline is printed on the notice you received, so find the date first. The federal ceiling is 90 days from the date the notice was mailed (see the Medicaid fine print), and a state can allow less. The SCDHHS appeals page also mentions a 30 day limit, but that one is for providers disputing a payment, not for members. A short, written request is enough to start, and the letters page has one.

A Way to Ask That Works

Ask in writing, keep a copy, and name the program you are asking about. The people who answer the phones are usually working through a long queue, and a clear question gets a clear answer. Family Connection of South Carolina is free and can help you work out which of these fits your child. Programs, limits and dates change, so check the SCDHHS pages linked below before you rely on a number here.
